Mayo Clinic / Senior AI Systems Engineer

AI infrastructure for clinical research

Current

Key resultPer-lab usage reporting helped raise sustained GPU utilization from under 50% to 80–85%; a reusable sandbox expanded access to agentic workloads on protected infrastructure.

On a five-person team, I operate three NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD clusters totaling 512 GPUs across 64 nodes, plus a separate 208-GPU general-purpose HPC system. We support more than 50 research labs running protein structure prediction, computational pathology, and memory-intensive genomics.

Scheduling that reflects actual demand

I built per-lab reporting from Slurm’s sacct and sreport, with continuous usage tracking. Putting those findings in front of labs helped them right-size their requests and sustain the improvement across clusters.

I also helped plan a Kubernetes and Run:ai cluster with project quotas, fair share, and fractional GPU sharing alongside the Slurm-based DGX systems.

Agentic AI on protected infrastructure

I built a bubblewrap sandbox that brings agentic workloads onto clinical infrastructure. A managed LLM gateway integration gives supported agents one-step setup, while labs can use the same primitive to wrap their own agents.

The sandbox is now open to most users across all clusters.

Storage, validation, and production readiness

I administer Lustre on Dell hardware for the B300 cluster and DDN storage for B200 systems. NCCL collective and Hugging Face model benchmarks validate sustained throughput before capacity reaches production research.

Repeatable provisioning and expansion

I planned and helped deliver Warewulf stateless provisioning for the general-purpose HPC system, making rebuilds repeatable ahead of H200 and high-memory expansion. I contributed to B200/B300 buildouts with Mark III Systems and NVIDIA field engineers, including network planning, configuration, and operating documentation.

Plain Theory Labs / Founder

An open measure of AI compute efficiency

Framework GitHub

Key resultReleased an open compute-efficiency framework with published weights, live telemetry ingestion, and validation against public cluster traces.

I founded Plain Theory Labs and released an open framework that produces a single deterministic efficiency score for AI compute clusters. The goal is to make assessments reproducible and grounded in collected data.

From telemetry to a reproducible score

Seven engines combine factors including GPU utilization, thermal efficiency, carbon accounting, scheduler policy, and hardware-to-workload fit. The framework ingests live telemetry from DCGM, Slurm, Kubernetes, and inference servers.

Open methodology and public-trace validation

I published the weights so results can be reproduced independently, and validated the scoring model against public cluster traces from MIT Supercloud, Alibaba Helios, and Microsoft Philly. Engines and methodology are published under MIT and CC BY 4.0.

University of Colorado Boulder / Research Computing

Expanding and automating campus HPC

2024–2025

Key resultLed the Alpine expansion with 64 GPUs and 2,176 CPU cores, alongside automated software deployment across several hundred research computing nodes.

I led the Alpine expansion, adding 64 NVIDIA GPUs across 16 nodes—eight H200 nodes and eight RTX Pro 6000 nodes—plus 17 CPU nodes with 2,176 cores. The expansion was sized to reduce AI and ML job wait times for the campus research community.

From facilities to institutional approval

I owned datacenter facility planning, Ceph storage architecture, Dell vendor negotiation, and budget modeling. I presented the proposal to institutional leadership to secure approval.

Configuration and deployment at scale

I led Puppet and Ansible configuration management across several hundred Linux nodes on Alpine and Blanca, a shared environment funded by three universities and the NSF. Git-driven pipelines triggered Ansible playbooks without cluster downtime, delivering new software stacks without a maintenance window.

GPU onboarding and validation

I brought L40S and H100 accelerators into production, tuning Slurm partitions, QoS, and CUDA configuration. TensorFlow and PyTorch benchmarks checked every new GPU node for driver and library compatibility and helped identify faulty cards early.

Reproducible research environments

Docker, Podman, and Kubernetes lab-cluster workflows replaced hand-built environment modules with versioned TensorFlow and PyTorch images. Researchers could pull working GPU stacks in minutes for genomics, climate modeling, and imaging.

University of Minnesota Brain Tumor Program / Lead Computational Biologist

Tracing tumor biology at single-cell resolution

Lab research

Key resultBuilt reproducible, GPU-accelerated genomics workflows spanning 100+ bulk RNA-sequencing patients and 21 single-nuclei samples.

Since 2019, I have run GPU-accelerated single-cell genomics to trace developmental lineages across glioma and chordoma cohorts, connecting computational methods with the biology of brain tumors.

GPU-accelerated models and sequencing pipelines

I train scVI and UniTVelo models in PyTorch and CUDA on Slurm clusters. I manage FASTQ data from BGI and 10x Genomics through Cell Ranger, STARsolo, and Kallisto, producing count matrices spanning 100+ bulk RNA-sequencing patients and 21 single-nuclei samples.

Multi-omics and reproducibility

I harmonize epigenomics and whole-genome sequencing datasets with collaborators at several universities. Pipelines are versioned in Nextflow and Git so analyses can be rerun reproducibly.
